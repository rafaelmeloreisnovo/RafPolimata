## 2. Contract architecture

The commercial relationship consists of, in descending order of conflict unless a signed Order expressly states otherwise:

1. mandatory applicable law;
2. signed Data Processing Addendum and mandatory transfer clauses for personal-data matters;
3. signed Order/Statement of Work (`SOW`);
4. this MSA;
5. Security Assurance Schedule;
6. accepted product/service documentation;
7. proposals, tickets and correspondence only to the extent expressly incorporated.

Third-party license terms govern the third-party components to which they apply and are not displaced by this MSA.

## 3. Services

Provider will perform only the services identified in a signed SOW. Each SOW must define scope, dependencies, exclusions, deliverables, acceptance criteria, environments, customer responsibilities, evidence requirements and change-control route.

`vision != artifact != execution != evidence != validated claim` is an operational contract invariant. A prototype, source file or successful synthetic test is not a production warranty unless the SOW expressly promotes that state after the stated gates.

## 4. Change control

Material changes to scope, architecture, personal-data processing, third-party providers, security posture, delivery dates, fees or acceptance criteria require a written change order or equivalent signed approval. Emergency security actions may be taken under the Security Schedule where necessary to contain harm, with post-event evidence and notification.

## 5. Customer dependencies

Customer will provide timely authorized access, accurate requirements, lawful datasets, credentials through approved secret channels, test environments and decisions necessary for the SOW. Provider is not responsible for delay caused by an unresolved customer dependency, but must record the dependency and minimize avoidable impact.

## 6. Fees, taxes and payment

Fees, currency, taxes, invoicing, payment term, expenses and late-payment rules are `TOKEN_VAZIO` until set in a signed SOW/Order. No public repository license implies free commercial support or production deployment rights.

## 7. Intellectual property

### 7.5 Third parties

## 8. Confidentiality

Each party will protect non-public information marked confidential or reasonably understood to be confidential using at least reasonable safeguards and only for contract purposes. Access is least-privilege and need-to-know. Exclusions normally include information demonstrably public without breach, already lawfully known, independently developed, or lawfully received without duty.

Legally compelled disclosure should, where permitted, be limited to what is required and preceded by reasonable notice to the other party when lawful.

## 9. Data protection

If Provider processes personal data on Customer's behalf, the DPA applies. The parties will document controller/processor or equivalent LGPD roles based on facts, not labels. Data processing, retention, location, subprocessors and international-transfer mechanisms must be completed before production processing.

## 10. Security

Provider will apply the controls selected in the Security Assurance Schedule for the contracted tier. Security obligations are risk-based and evidence-backed. No clause is a guarantee that incidents or vulnerabilities can never occur.

Customer must promptly report suspected compromise affecting the service and maintain security of Customer-controlled credentials, endpoints, users and configurations.

## 11. Vulnerabilities and incidents

The parties will follow the incident-response and vulnerability process in the Security Schedule. Provider may suspend or isolate a component where reasonably necessary to prevent material harm, preserving evidence and restoration/rollback options where feasible.

## 12. Service levels and support

Availability, response targets, support hours, maintenance windows, RTO/RPO and service credits are not universal defaults. They are enforceable only when selected in a signed SOW/SLA. Measurement method and exclusions must be defined with each metric.

## 13. Acceptance

Acceptance must be tied to objective SOW criteria. Unless the SOW provides a different period, a deliverable is not deemed accepted merely because source was transmitted. Customer must be given the agreed verification opportunity. Rejection must identify the unmet criterion with enough detail to reproduce it.

## 14. Warranties

Provider warrants only that it will perform professional services with reasonable skill and care and that it has authority to grant the rights expressly granted. Technical or performance warranties, if any, must be stated in the SOW.

Except for express warranties and to the extent permitted by law, materials and experimental/research components are provided without implied warranties. Mandatory consumer or statutory rights are not excluded.

## 15. Indemnity

Any IP, data-protection, confidentiality or third-party indemnity must identify scope, exclusions, control of defense, cooperation and remedies in the commercial Order. No unlimited indemnity is inferred from this template.

Provider remains responsible for contractual flow-down obligations to the extent required by applicable law and the signed agreement.

## 20. Compliance with law

Each party is responsible for laws applicable to its own role. Neither party instructs the other to violate applicable privacy, security, export/sanctions, anti-corruption, labor, intellectual-property or sector-specific law.

## 21. Suspension

Provider may proportionately suspend affected functionality for material security threat, unlawful use, non-payment after required notice, or material breach where suspension is contractually/lawfully available. Emergency suspension should be as narrow as reasonably feasible and subject to restoration review.
